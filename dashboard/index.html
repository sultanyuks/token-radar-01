How the implied value is priced

The snapshot prices its own future launches. Of its 758 protocols, the ones whose token already trades carry both a TVL and a market cap, and the spread of their market-cap/TVL ratios is the market's own answer to “what is a dollar of TVL worth once it has a token?”. Each pre-token protocol is priced at its category's median ratio — and shown with the band between its category's 25th and 75th percentile ratios, so a price never pretends to be more certain than the peers behind it:

implied value = TVL × category median multiple × momentum factor × seasoning factor
implied range = TVL × category quartile multiple × momentum factor × seasoning factor
implied per $1 farmed = implied value ÷ the farm capital the row's own plan commits
halving / doubling multiple = the peer multiple at which the implied value halves / doubles

  • Peer band. Each category percentile is shrunk geometrically toward the global one with weight peers/(peers+8): a category with forty trading peers keeps its own spread; one with two leans on the market's. A category with no trading peers is priced on the global band and says so.
  • Momentum factor. Gains and losses are not symmetric evidence — a doubling of TVL in a week can be rented with an incentive, a halving is rarely faked. Gains pass through at 0.50 (clamped at +100%, so at most ×1.50), losses at 0.90 (clamped at −50%, so at least ×0.55). An unreported 7-day change is not read as zero: the factor is 1 and the row is flagged.
  • Seasoning factor = 0.80 + 0.45 × √(min(age, 1,460 days) / 1,460). Concave: the premium for accrued, unpriced activity arrives early — the second tokenless year says less than the first. An unrecorded listing date is priced at the median age of the ranked protocols whose dates are recorded, and the row is flagged.
  • Confidence follows the evidence behind the band: deep (10+ trading peers), solid (4–9), thin (1–3), global (none — priced on the market-wide band).
  • Farm efficiency. A ranking by implied value alone cannot say where a farming dollar works hardest, because each row's plan commits a different amount: $50 against a small protocol, $500 against a giant (the tiers are in the farm-plans note below). So every row also carries its implied value per $1 of the farm capital its own plan commits, and the board sorts by it. Read it beside the evidence grade, never instead of it: the highest per-$ figures sit on small protocols, where the band behind the price is often thin — efficiency on thin evidence is a lead, not a conclusion.
  • What the market must believe. The price is linear in the peer multiple — momentum and seasoning are the protocol's own record, not the market's mood — so each detail view states the multiple at which the price halves and the one at which it doubles, and where those lines fall against the category's own band. If the halving line sits below the band's low end, even the pessimistic quarter of peers leaves the price more than half intact; if the doubling line sits inside the band, the category's stronger quarter already trades there. The sensitivity is stated in the market's own units, from the row's own numbers.
  • The peers are auditable. A band is a claim about a list, so every detail view carries the list: its category's trading peers by name, each with the market cap, TVL and market-cap/TVL ratio it contributes, sorted by ratio, the peers inside the shrunk band shaded. The quartiles above are computed from exactly those ratios (before shrinkage toward the global band) — a reader who doubts a multiple can recount it from the same page that states it. A row priced on the global band shows the market-wide list instead.
  • Risk flags. The cautions below, each computed from the snapshot alone and each with a stated trigger, ride under a row's name on the board and are defined again in its detail view. Thin evidence: the category band rests on 1–3 trading peers. Global band: the category has no trading peer at all, so the market-wide band prices the row. Single chain: every dollar of its TVL sits on one chain. Young: a recorded listing date under 180 days ago. 7d unreported: no 7-day change in the record, so its momentum factor is 1 by construction, not by observation. Flags never move a price — they say how far to trust it, and a row with no flag is not safe, only unflagged on these four tests.
  • Farm plans are written for the cheapest chain the protocol itself runs on, with gas quoted for that chain: the same position costs about $10.50 to set up on Ethereum and about $0.25 on Base, and a plan that hides that is not a plan. The position each plan commits is sized from the protocol's own TVL — $50 under $10M, $100 to $150 through the middle tiers, $250 at $1B, $500 at $5B and beyond — because a plan sized without regard to the protocol's depth is not a plan either. A protocol whose TVL fell 15% or more in the week gets a safety-check step before any deposit; one that grew 40% or more gets a sizing caution.
  • No name is special-cased. The same function scores every row, and every multiple is recomputed from the snapshot at run time — re-run on a future snapshot, the same program finds future launches early.
  • Pre-token means “records no token” in DefiLlama's record (no market cap, no CoinGecko id, no symbol) — a claim about the record, never proof a token does not exist. Trading protocols sit in the Live view at the market's own values, never mixed into this ranking.
